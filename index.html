Horizon Scanner

Measure your skyline (trees, buildings and ridges) with the phone camera and orientation sensors, then export a horizon.txt file for the Open-Meteo Solar Forecast integration.

This page isn't running in a secure context. Browsers only allow camera and compass access over HTTPS (or on localhost). Open it from an HTTPS address, for example your Home Assistant under https://…/local/horizon-scanner.html, or GitHub Pages. Another option: in Chrome on Android, open chrome://flags/#unsafely-treat-insecure-origin-as-secure and add this page's http:// origin.

How to measure

  1. Stand at the middle of your panel array, at panel height if you can.
  2. Keep away from metal (panel frames, railings, a car) because it throws off the compass. Wave the phone in a figure-8 once to calibrate it.
  3. Put the red crosshair on the skyline, then hold “Trace” and slowly sweep along the tops of trees, roofs and ridges. Tap “+ Point” for single spots such as a chimney tip.
  4. Cover at least east → south → west, which is everywhere the sun goes. To be thorough, sweep north-east → north-west.
  5. Use Align to sun on a sunny day for the most accurate compass. It removes magnetic declination and nearby interference in one step.
  6. Open Results, check the chart against the sun paths, tap any bad points to delete them, then download the file.

Settings

Declination is about +6° in western Ukraine. Check the NOAA magnetic field calculator for your exact spot. You can skip it if you use “Align to sun”. Location is used only on this phone, for the sun position and the sun-path chart.

Align to sun

Put the sun in the center of the crosshair. Look at the screen, never at the sun itself. Hold steady, then tap Sun centered.

horizon.txt

Copy the file to your Home Assistant config folder, for example /config/horizon.txt. Then go to the integration's Configure → array page → tick Use horizon file and set the path. Directions you didn't measure are interpolated in a straight line between their measured neighbours.
